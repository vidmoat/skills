# Trailer Editor

Cuts a trailer: three acts, a rising build, a title card, and a button people quote.

This is the brief the Trailer Editor gives when the user tags @trailer-editor or the planner activates this skill. It is professional advice inside the user's brief: the user's goal, explicit exclusions, brand rules, access and spending limits win. Its numbers are proposed targets; check that each applies and can be measured before using it. For an evidence-grounded shot plan from this specialist, use the specialist-consultation workflow (agentWorkspace action specialist_consult) instead of reading the brief alone.

Work the DERIVE list out first, from the clips actually on the timeline, and say the figures you derived. Before calling a step done, check it against the ACCEPT line.

## Brief

INTENT: three acts, a rising build, and a button.
SPEC: Act 1 setup, Act 2 conflict and escalation, Act 3 montage build → title card → BUTTON. Feature trailers run to 2:00 under the current exhibition guideline (down from the older 2:30 convention); teasers 60-90s. Cut lengths per act: 2.0-3.0s, then 1.0-1.5s, then halving (1.0 / 0.5 / 0.25 / 0.13s) into one hard stop.
DERIVE FIRST: work these out from the timeline in front of you, then apply the SPEC to those figures:
· Total available footage vs the target length, which sets the act boundaries: roughly 35% setup, 30% escalation, 35% build plus title plus button.
· The cut-length schedule as real durations for each act, computed from that.
· Which clip is the strongest single image: that is the cold open, and it is not usually the first one on the timeline.
CRAFT
· THE BUTTON is the ~5 seconds AFTER the main title: one joke, one scare, one line, or a smash to the logo. It is the most-quoted part of the cut and amateur trailers do not have one.
· The device kit, by name: braams and hits, risers, whooshes, sub-drops, flash frames, smash cuts, text cards in threes with rising cadence, speed ramps into a hit.
· DROP OUT to near-silence for 1-2s before the title lands. The gap is what makes the hit enormous: gain is not.
· Trailer music is itself three-act. The music's structure should BE the picture's structure, not a bed underneath it.
· Never reveal the third act. Sell the question, not the answer.
· Text cards: 4 words maximum, one per beat of the build.
DO NOT: reveal the ending; fade the music out; use a dissolve anywhere; text cards longer than 4 words; a title card at the very end with nothing after it.
ACCEPT: the silence gap before the title measures ≥1.4s, and a button exists after the title card.

## Gotchas

- The silence before the title must exist in the mix, not just in the plan: duck or cut every audio clip across that gap and check it with the audio review.
- Never reveal the third act; sell the question.
