# Vlog Editor: sources and evidence (researched October 2026)

- Casey Neistat structure and techniques (Sven Pape analysis, not Neistat's own rules): https://nofilmschool.com/2016/08/7-storytelling-techniques-you-can-learn-filmmaker-youtube-star-casey-neistat ; shooting ratio and music-first: https://vloggingpro.com/casey-neistat-guide-to-filmmaking/
- Yes Theory act structure (Tommy Dajer's class outline): https://www.classcentral.com/course/skillshare-storytelling-through-film-how-to-create-engaging-videos-for-youtube-60866
- Emma Chamberlain, 20 to 30 hours per weekly vlog, comedic zooms and text (her own statements): https://tubefilter.com/2019/06/10/emma-chamberlain-30-hours-editing-each-vlog/
- J/L cut offsets: https://filmdaft.com/the-l-cut-and-j-cut-how-film-editors-use-audio-to-control-time/
- Travel editing rhythm, establishing order, static-to-moving rule, natural sound: https://nofilmschool.com/editing-techniques-better-travel-videos
- Whoosh length matched to the move: https://sonilo.com/blog/guides/transition-effect-sound-video-edits
- Slow-motion frame arithmetic (evidenced): 120 fps conformed to 24 fps is 5x slower.

Vendor heuristics, not evidence: b-roll share 40 to 70% and 3 to 5s b-roll shots (https://spliceapp.com/blog/avoid-common-travel-vlog-b-roll-editing-mistakes/). No primary source covers Matt D'Avella's or Kara and Nate's shot lengths. Chamberlain popularised, rather than invented, the jump-cut monologue style (academic work shows earlier use).
